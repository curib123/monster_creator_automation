## What to carry into the sprites

| Feature | Reference observations and production direction |
| --- | --- |
| Texture | Finish each surface in the reference's polished 2D anime-fantasy style. White fur forms layered tapered locks with pale lavender or blue shadows. Dark plates use overlapping planes, beveled ridges and crisp reflected edges. Fine fluid streaks, frosted fin edges and small specular glints give water volume. Crystals use distinct facets and colored refraction, and metal accents use tight warm highlights. Translate these material cues to the family's own anatomy and element. |
| Shading and lighting | Establish a clear key from above and the front, then use attached elemental materials to tint nearby planes. Build shadows in distinct groups, soften transitions on rounded forms, and reserve sharp value changes for facets, ridges, overlaps and point highlights. Keep emissive rim light local enough that ears, paws, wings and other contours remain readable. |
| Outlines and edges | Use tapered, hue-shifted outlines with deeper values at overlaps and in shadow. Let bright or translucent shapes resolve against their surroundings with softer edges where appropriate. Keep a clean, antialiased alpha edge and introduce small internal marks where they describe a surface or direction of flow. |
| Color | The top row contrasts cream-white fur, violet shadows, pink-violet crystal glow and warm metallic trim. The middle row layers aqua, cyan and cobalt with white foam-like highlights and a small warm accent. The bottom row sets warm ivory plates against deep plum and violet with a magenta core. Each palette concentrates saturation near a focal area and keeps quieter body planes for contrast. Use each family's own element colors and value structure. |
| Faces and expressions | Large, glassy eyes with iris gradients, small concentrated glints and clean brow lines make young faces readable. The upper row is softly alert, the middle row playful with open smiles, and the bottom row becomes more intent as the brow and muzzle develop. Use expression appropriate to the archetype, including a clear center of attention for creatures with unconventional faces. |
| Anatomy and proportions | Early forms use a dominant head and compact body. The intermediate forms develop stance, joints or appendage structure. Final forms have clearer necks, limbs or equivalent anatomy and a more deliberate silhouette. Carry family anchors through every stage, such as the ear placement and tail flow in the upper row, fin and crest rhythm in the middle row, and plate shapes and muzzle structure in the bottom row. Keep the anatomy of the chosen archetype recognizable. |
| Pose | Use a full-body, left-facing front three-quarter view. Aim the muzzle, beak, face or leading feature screen-left and show enough of the far side to establish depth. The sheet varies its poses, including the right-looking final sprite in the top row. Use the sheet for painting and lineage cues while enforcing the consistent left-facing production angle. |

## From a reference sheet to a playable sprite

Use the verified master as actual image conditioning. Attach an accepted family
reference when the pending request calls for one. Design new characters with
the same rendering language and their own shapes, markings, palette and motifs.
The sheet's number badges, shared composition, framing auras and repeated
ornaments are sheet features. Deliver one full body per sprite, with clear
transparent padding and no text, panels, detached decoration or ground shadow.
Favor a compact elemental tail, fin, plume, grown plate or attached light over
effects that hide anatomy or muddy the silhouette. Preserve intentional
translucency on edges and materials. Check the art at sprite scale as well as
full resolution on light and dark backgrounds.

Use the [evolution and production gate](evolution-quality.md) for stage and
family evaluation. Apply this standard to every Wild, Legendary and Guardian
family across all 25 worlds.
